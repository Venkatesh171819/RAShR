Repeated Angular Shorth Regression

Robust line fitting by asking which direction do pairwise chords agree on? — not which line has small residuals?

Based on “Robust Linear Regression via the Repeated Angular Shorth” — V. Dharavath & V. Srivastava. Use ← → keys, or the ☰ menu. Slides with sliders are interactive.

The problem

Robust methods can still follow the wrong line

100 points: 62% follow $y=1+2x$; 38% form a compact cluster at a high-leverage position.

OLS, LMS and MM all return negative slopes. RAShR returns +2.02 in the paper's example (+2.02 in the paper; our re-created sample gives a similar value).

Why is this surprising? These estimators have a breakdown point near ½ — they cannot be driven to infinity. But “bounded” is not the same as “follows the majority trend”.
Terminology — built up step by step

From data to coherent contamination

Outlier vs contamination

Outlier: an unusual single point. Contamination: observations that do not follow the main pattern — possibly many.

Coherent contamination

Contaminated points related to each other: a compact cluster or a competing line. Ordinary contamination is scattered arbitrarily.

High leverage

A point whose $x$ is far from the bulk of the $x$'s. It has a strong effect on the slope. Ordinary contamination can sit at normal $x$.

Breakdown point

Fraction of observations that can be replaced by arbitrary values before the estimate can be driven arbitrarily far. A worst-case boundedness guarantee.

Residual / residual-based

Residual: $y_i-\hat y_i$ (observed minus predicted). Residual-based method: fits by judging candidate lines through the size of residuals (LMS, LTS, MM…).

Two desirable properties

(1) estimate stays bounded; (2) estimate follows the line supported by the dominant structure. High breakdown gives (1), not automatically (2).

Residual view

A narrow strip through the cluster looks attractive

LMS looks for the narrowest vertical strip containing half the data. A line through a compact cluster only needs a little extra mass from the majority to reach 50%.

$W(\ell)=\inf\{w:\Pr(|Y-\ell(X)|\le w)\ge\tfrac12\}$

Population statement (Proposition 3, shown later): as contamination $\varepsilon\uparrow\tfrac12$ a line through a point-mass cluster achieves $W(\ell)\to0$. The strip around the true line always needs positive width because of noise.

The central idea

Change the question

Instead of asking “Which line produces small residuals?”
RAShR asks “Which direction receives the strongest agreement from pairwise chords?”
data→chords→chord directions→angular concentration→shorth→repeated angular shorth→RAShR

Majority–majority chords

Seen from many majority points, they all point roughly along the true line → concentrated angles.

Majority–cluster chords

The cluster is viewed from many different places → directions spread over a wide range.

Consequence

A cluster that is very influential in residual space need not dominate in direction space.

RAShR — the central section

Roadmap

points→standardize→chords→directions φ→projective angles→ angular shorth→local directions θᵢ, wᵢ→repeated shorth θ̂→slope β̂→intercept α̂→line

1 · Put axes on comparable scales

Angles depend on units, so standardize robustly first.

2 · Dominant direction per anchor

Each observation looks at all its chords and finds where half of them concentrate.

3 · Combine local directions

The same concentration operation, applied again across anchors.

Step 1

Angles depend on units

Changing $x$ from metres to centimetres changes the numerical angle of the same relationship. So standardize first:

$$u_i=\frac{x_i-\operatorname{med}(x)}{s_x},\quad v_i=\frac{y_i-\operatorname{med}(y)}{s_y},\quad s=1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}$$

If a MAD is zero, the standard deviation of that coordinate is used. 1.4826 gives normal-consistent scaling.

original data → robust centre (median) → robust scale (MAD) → standardized data.

Step 5

The direction an anchor sees

$$\Phi_i=\{\phi_{ij}:j\ne i\},\quad \theta_i=\mathrm{ASh}(\Phi_i),\quad w_i=w_{\lceil (n-1)/2\rceil}(\Phi_i)$$

$\theta_i$ is the dominant direction anchor $i$ sees; $w_i$ is the width of its shortest half-window.

Small $w_i$ → strong directional agreement. Large $w_i$ → weak agreement. Repeat for all anchors → $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_n$.

Step 6

Do it again, across anchors

$$\hat\theta=\mathrm{ASh}\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_n\}$$

Local aggregation

inside each anchor's $n-1$ chord directions ($h_L=\lceil (n-1)/2\rceil$)

Outer aggregation

across the $n$ local directions ($h_O=\lceil n/2\rceil$)

That's why it is Repeated Angular Shorth Regression. The outer width is kept as a diagnostic, together with the $w_i$.

Step 7

Direction → slope → intercept → line

standardized direction $\hat\theta$→$\tan\hat\theta$ (standardized slope)→undo $y$ scale: $\times s_y$→undo $x$ scale: $\div s_x$→$\hat\beta$
$$\hat\beta=\frac{s_y}{s_x}\tan\hat\theta\ \ (\hat\theta\in(-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2]),\qquad \hat\alpha=\operatorname{med}_i\big(y_i-\hat\beta x_i\big),\qquad \hat y=\hat\alpha+\hat\beta x$$

Why the median for $\alpha$?

Lines with the same slope $\hat\beta$ slide vertically; each observation implies an intercept $y_i-\hat\beta x_i$. The median picks the robust vertical placement.

Honest caveat

The intercept uses all observations. Under heavy contamination (e.g. 38% at $(15,-10)$) the median intercept can be pulled even when $\hat\beta$ is right — the paper's evaluation concerns the slope.

Algorithm 1

The whole procedure

raw observations↓robust standardization↓pairwise chord directions↓local shortest half-window↓local directions↓second angular shorth↓final direction↓original-scale slope↓median intercept↓final line
(u,v) ← robust standardization of (x,y) [Eq.2]
for i = 1..n:
  Φᵢ ← { atan2(v_j−v_i, u_j−u_i) mod π : j≠i }
  sort Φᵢ, append copy shifted by π
  narrowest window with ⌈q(n−1)⌉ angles → θᵢ (midpoint), wᵢ (width)
same window search on {θ₁..θₙ} with ⌈qn⌉ → θ̂, w
β̂ ← (s_y/s_x)·tan θ̂ ; α̂ ← med(yᵢ − β̂xᵢ)
Cost. $n$ anchors × sort of $n-1$ angles: $O(n^2\log n)$ time, $O(n)$ extra memory if anchors are processed one at a time — same order as a direct repeated median, and cheaper than exhaustive elemental-subset search for LMS/LTS, which is $O(n^3)$ in the paper's implementations.

Deterministic: ties go to the smallest left endpoint; no random sampling.

Proposition 1

Exact fit

Statement. If $y_i=\alpha^\star+\beta^\star x_i$ with distinct $x_i$, then $(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta)=(\alpha^\star,\beta^\star)$.

Geometry. Every chord has the same direction $\theta^\star$ ⇒ all local and outer shorth widths are zero ⇒ $\hat\theta=\theta^\star$ ⇒ exact slope and intercept.

Corollary 1 (exact fit under contamination). If the majority lies exactly on a line, no contaminated point is on it, and $|B|<\lceil (n-1)/2\rceil$, then the line is recovered regardless of where the contamination is. A group smaller than the half-sample size cannot form a competing zero-width window. (Shared with LMS and the repeated median — it is not unique to RAShR.)
Proposition 2

Translation and positive-scale equivariance

$$x'=a_x+b_xx,\ y'=a_y+b_yy,\ b_x,b_y>0\ \Rightarrow\ \hat\beta'=\frac{b_y}{b_x}\hat\beta$$

Robust standardization removes translations and positive rescalings, so the same chords and the same shorths are selected; only the back-transformation changes the slope.

Limitation. RAShR is not fully affine equivariant: $y\to y+\gamma x$ also changes the robust $y$-scale $s_y$, so it is not a simple rotation of the standardized data (right panel).

A tight group beats a separated group

While the contaminated angles stay separated, the shortest half-arc ignores them exactly. Once they enter the majority arc (or make a competing arc narrower), the estimate moves.

Conditions (L) and (O)

Two levels of separation

(L) Local separation

At every majority anchor, the shortest half-interval formed by majority–majority chords is narrower than any competing interval of the same size involving a contaminated chord. ⇒ $\theta_i$ at majority anchors depends only on majority chords.

(O) Outer separation

Among the local directions, the concentrated majority-anchor directions form a narrower half-sample interval than any competing interval involving contaminated-anchor directions.

Size condition. $|G|>n/2$ ensures $|G|-1\ge h_L$ and $|G|\ge h_O$: both stages can be formed from majority observations alone.

Majority anchors (blue) form the large concentrated group; contaminated anchors (red) form a smaller group at another direction — that is (L)+(O) in action.

Proposition 3

Why coherent contamination can attract LMS

Model: $(X,Y)\sim(1-\varepsilon)P_G+\varepsilon\,\delta_c$, $P_G:Y=\alpha^\star+\beta^\star X+e$, $e\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, point mass at distance $\Delta>0$ from the majority line.

Statement. There is $\varepsilon_0<\tfrac12$ such that for every $\varepsilon\in(\varepsilon_0,\tfrac12)$ every population LMS solution satisfies $|c_y-\ell(c_x)|\le W(\ell)$ with $W(\ell)\to0$ as $\varepsilon\uparrow\frac12$. The majority line is not an LMS solution.

Scope. Proved for population LMS only. For LTS and MM the paper provides empirical evidence (Section 5), not a theorem. Also: the result is asymptotic in $\varepsilon\uparrow\tfrac12$.

Proof idea. A strip excluding $c$ and holding half the mass needs half-width $\ge w_{1/4}>0$ (Anderson's inequality); a strip through $c$ already holds mass $\varepsilon$ and needs only $\tfrac12-\varepsilon\to0$ more from the majority.

Remark 1

Why we need a concentrated half, not all chords

A clean-looking sufficient condition: every majority–majority chord lies within $\delta<\pi/8$ of $\theta^\star$, giving $d_\pi(\hat\theta,\theta^\star)\le4\delta$.

It is valid mathematics but useless for noisy data: two points with nearly equal $x$ and a little vertical noise give a chord with an almost arbitrary direction (red chords on the left).

RAShR needs the half-sample to concentrate — which is what the shorth detects — not every chord.
Section 5

Simulation design

Base model $n=100$, $x\sim U[-5,5]$, $y=1+2x+e$, $e\sim N(0,1)$. A fraction $f$ is replaced by:

  1. compact leverage cluster at $(15,-10)$, Gaussian spread 0.3
  2. competing line $y=5-1.25x+e_c$, $e_c\sim N(0,0.5^2)$, same $x$-range
  3. funnel: heteroscedastic majority (sd $0.2+0.6|x|$) + competing line (sd 0.3)

Metrics. Accuracy $|\hat\beta-2|$; failure $\Pr(|\hat\beta-2|>0.5)$; 60 replications per point in the paper.

LTS: ten best elemental fits + 20 concentration steps. MM: LTS start, MAD scale, Tukey bisquare. RANSAC: two-point samples, MAD-based threshold, 1000 trials (deliberately many).

Reproducibility note. The curves on the next slides come from our re-implementation of all five estimators (deterministic seeds). Some tuning constants (notably RANSAC's threshold) are not fully specified in the paper, so we compare patterns, and list the paper's own reported numbers separately (◇).

Boundaries

Direction and compactness

A 38% cluster was placed in twelve directions at two distances. RAShR had no failures in ten of twelve; the two difficult directions are ≈30° and −150°, where the cluster lies close to the extension of the majority line. Reported at distance 30: failure 78% (30°) and 75% (−150°).

On the projective circle, 30° and −150° are the same unoriented direction ($-150^\circ+180^\circ=30^\circ$): chords to the cluster and majority–majority chords overlap in angle, so the separation is lost.

Compactness. Spread 0.05: RAShR no failures; LMS 25%, LTS and MM ≈98% (paper). Spread 1 or 3: the cluster is no longer a compact coherent group, and LMS, LTS, MM and RAShR all fail in at most 2% of samples. The advantage is specific to compact coherent contamination, not arbitrary outliers. Same compact-cluster behaviour for $n\in\{40,60,200,400\}$.

Summary

When is RAShR useful?

robust regression problem→residual methods can follow coherent contamination→pairwise chord directions→projective angles→shorth→repeated angular shorth→robust direction → line

Use it when

  • ≈35–46% of the data form a compact, coherent structure
  • the structure is angularly separated from the majority line
  • you want a deterministic estimator

Prefer MM / RANSAC when

  • data are clean or lightly contaminated
  • contamination is arbitrary or diffuse
  • efficiency matters most

Known limits

  • cluster on the line's extension (≈30°, −150°)
  • compact cluster at 46%: all methods fail
  • not fully affine equivariant; scales still use all data
